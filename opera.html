Pierce Wand Light Opera

H.M.S. PINAFORE - ACT I - ACT II

ACT I — Scene.— The Quarter-deck of H.M.S. Pinafore, off Portsmouth. Sailors, led by Boatswain, discovered cleaning brasswork, splicing ropes, etc.


BOAT.

We sail the ocean blue,
And our saucy ship’s a beauty;
We’re sober men and true,
And attentive to our duty.
When the balls whistle free
O’er the bright blue sea,
We stand to our guns all day;
When at anchor we ride
On the Portsmouth tide,
We’ve plenty of time for play.


CHORUS.

Aye, aye, play!


BOAT.

When at anchor we ride
On the Portsmouth tide,
We’ve plenty of time for play!


(Enter Little Buttercup, a Portsmouth bumboat woman, with a large basket of wares.)


BUT.

RECITATIVE. For I’m called Little Buttercup—dear Little Buttercup,
Though I could never tell why,
But still I’m called Buttercup—poor Little Buttercup,
Sweet Little Buttercup I!


SONG. I’m called Little Buttercup, dear Little Buttercup,
Though I could never tell why,
But still I’m called Buttercup—poor Little Buttercup,
Sweet Little Buttercup I!
I’ve snuff and tobaccy, and excellent jacky,
I’ve scissors, and watches, and knives;
I’ve ribbons and laces to set off the faces
Of pretty young sweethearts and wives.


CHORUS.

Aye, aye, wives!


BUT.

I’ve treacle and toffee, I’ve tea and I’ve coffee,
Soft tommy and succulent chops;
I’ve chickens and conies, and pretty polonies,
And excellent peppermint drops.


CHORUS.

Aye, aye, drops!


BOAT.

RECITATIVE. My gallant crew, good morning.


ALL.

Sir, good morning!


BOAT.

I hope you’re all quite well.


ALL.

Quite well; and you?


BOAT.

I am, in reasonable health, and happy
To meet you all once more.


ALL.

You’re very kind.


(Enter RALPH RACKSTRAW, who touches his cap to the Boatswain.)


RAL.

RECITATIVE. I am the Captain of the Pinafore!


ALL.

(astonished) Sir, you!


RAL.

Yes—I am the Captain of the Pinafore!


(CAPTAIN CORCORAN enters.)


CAPT.

And I—the Captain of the Pinafore!


[Here follows a lengthy scene of dialogue and song, leading to the entrance of Sir Joseph Porter, K.C.B., with his sisters and his cousins and his aunts.]


JOS.

RECITATIVE. I am the monarch of the sea,
The ruler of the Queen’s Navee,
Whose praise Great Britain loudly chants!


COUSINS & AUNTS.

And we are his sisters, and his cousins, and his aunts!


JOS.

When at anchor here I ride,
My bosom swells with pride,
And I snap my fingers at a foeman’s taunts!


COUSINS & AUNTS.

And so do his sisters, and his cousins, and his aunts!


JOS.

SONG. When I was a lad I served a term
As office boy to an attorney’s firm.
I cleaned the windows and I swept the floor,
And I polished up the handle of the big front door.


CHORUS.

He polished up the handle of the big front door!


JOS.

I polished up that handle so carefullee
That now I am the ruler of the Queen’s Navee!



ACT II — Same scene. Moonlight.


(Captain Corcoran picks up a guitar and sings a sad song. Little Buttercup appears.)


CAPT.

SONG. Fair moon, to thee I sing,
Bright regent of the heavens,
Say, why is everything
Either at sixes or at sevens?


(BUTTERCUP enters unobserved; she listens.)


BUT.

RECITATIVE. I can’t tell why, to me,
She’s very much too plain,
And yet his captain he
Is bidding her in vain!


CAPT.

Ah, Little Buttercup, on what errand come you?


BUT.

I have come to say goodbye, Sir Joseph and the crew are going ashore.


CAPT.

Goodbye? but you’ll come back, I trust?


BUT.

SONG. Things are seldom what they seem,
Skim milk masquerades as cream;
Highlows pass as patent leathers;
Jackdaws strut in peacock feathers.


CAPT.

(puzzled) Very true — but what means that at present?


[Exeunt, with moonlight effect. – End of excerpt.]
